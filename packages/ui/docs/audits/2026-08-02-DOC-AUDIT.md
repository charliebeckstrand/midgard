# Documentation Audit — TSDoc coverage and controlled-language conformance

**Date:** 2026-08-02 · **Scope:** every symbol the `ui` barrels re-export (136 barrels, 1233 exports). Also the curated surface docs under [`docs/`](..) and the hub [`REFERENCE.md`](../../REFERENCE.md). **Method:** a TypeScript compiler-API scan walked each barrel's export chain. It reported the doccomment on every hop, so a doc on an intermediate export specifier counts. A second pass linted the collected doccomments and the Markdown for ASD-STE100 rule breaks. **Living record — resolve rows in place with the pull request that closes them.**

## Executive summary

Coverage was already strong and the surface indexes were structurally sound. The package documented 1196 of 1233 public exports before this sweep, and the component, module, and primitive indexes named every directory they index. The defect sat in two places. 36 exports carried no doccomment. The prose across both TSDoc and Markdown drifted from the controlled language ([`STE.md`](../../../../STE.md)).

The sweep closed every coverage gap and every index gap. It closed the whole modal-verb category (rule 10) across `src`, and it closed the long-sentence and passive-voice categories in the living Markdown. The same two categories stay open across most TSDoc. They are a per-sentence rewrite of 453 sites, and this sweep only reduced the densest files.

One scope decision frames the open rows. The `-ing` category (rule 5) is out of scope by direction. It holds about 840 sites. Most are participial adjectives — "the *enclosing* dialog", "the *underlying* seam" — and a rewrite of those reads worse than the original. Part 2 of the standard settles which of those the Dictionary approves, and this repository does not hold the Dictionary ([`STE.md`](../../../../STE.md)).

## Method

The coverage scan resolved each barrel export through `getImmediateAliasedSymbol` and collected the doccomment at every hop of the re-export chain. The first two passes under-reported, and the numbers below come from the third. A doccomment sits in one of three places. It sits on an export specifier in the slot re-export pattern (`export { Body as DialogBody }`), on the statement of a destructured `createContext` pair, or on the final declaration. A scan that reads only the final declaration reports a false gap for the first two.

The language pass linted the collected doccomments and the Markdown separately. The Markdown pass measures a table cell as its own descriptive unit. A scan that splits a table row on sentence punctuation counts the heading and the column scaffold as sentence text. It then reports a false break of rule 6.

## Open

**453 sentences over the rule 6 limit, across 234 files.** Rule 6 caps an instruction at 20 words and a descriptive sentence at 25. Each remaining site is a per-sentence rewrite. The top files by count are `hooks/use-plot-frame.ts` (11), `modules/grid/grid-data-types.ts` (11), `modules/grid/types.ts` (10), `primitives/density/density.tsx` (7), `modules/map/map-range-legend.tsx` (7), `components/menu/menu-sub.tsx` (7), `utilities/color-scale.ts` (6), and `modules/grid/engine/grid-export/types.ts` (6).

**161 passive constructions across 113 files, of which about 128 are real.** Rule 4 bans the passive voice in an instruction and permits it in descriptive text only where the active voice is longer or less clear. This category therefore needs a per-site call, not a blanket rewrite. The linter's count includes adjectival predicates that are not passives at all: `is required`, `is omitted`, and `is mounted` read as state, not as an action. The count rose by two in #1041, where two correctness fixes chose a clearer passive over a clumsy active — the exception rule 4 allows. The densest files are `modules/chat/use-chat-send.ts` (5), `hooks/use-plot-frame.ts` (4), `components/markdown/markdown.tsx` (4), `components/kanban/context.ts` (4), and `components/file-upload/file-upload.tsx` (4).

**The `-ing` category (rule 5), about 843 sites.** Out of scope for this sweep by direction. Reopen it only with the Dictionary in hand. Part 2 approves a subset of these words, and a rewrite without it degrades the prose it touches.

## Ruled out

**The component, module, and primitive indexes are not stale.** [`COMPONENTS.md`](../COMPONENTS.md) names all 97 component directories, [`MODULES.md`](../MODULES.md) all 5, and [`PRIMITIVES.md`](../PRIMITIVES.md) all 22. A symbol-level scan reports hundreds of missing names against these three documents, because they index a directory for each entry by design (§12.2). The next audit does not need to walk this ground.

**`useFormActions` is documented.** An early scan reported it as a gap. Its doccomment sits above a lone `export { useFormActions }` clause at `components/form/context.tsx`, which the first alias walk did not reach. `tsdoc-coverage-boundary.test.ts` now accepts that placement, and the surface reports 1233 of 1233.

**The stale-looking names in `HOOKS.md` are third-party references.** `useFloating`, `useId`, `Range`, and `useSortable` name floating-ui, React, the DOM, and dnd-kit. `useMaskInput` names an internal hook that no barrel exports, referenced in prose as the caller of `useFormattedInput`.

**`audits/` and `plans/` stay in their authored voice.** They hold 376 rule breaks between them, excluding the `-ing` category. An audit is a point-in-time record that gets deleted once its findings close, and a plan is a permanent historical record of a decision (§12.4). Neither is a living surface document, so neither is worth the churn.

## Surfaced, not fixed

`src/components/stack/stack.tsx` carries an open design question in its `@remarks`, marked "Unresolved by design, pending a maintainer call". Read it there; the remark outlives this audit. Two outcomes settle it. Either Stack is a strict column, which type-locks `direction` and migrates the eight demos that pass `direction="row"`. Or it is a col-defaulted Flex, and the Flex doc must say so. This sweep made the remark conform to rule 10 and changed nothing about its substance. The call is a maintainer's, and it belongs to one change that settles both documents.
